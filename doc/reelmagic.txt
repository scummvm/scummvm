ReelMagic support: provenance and hardware observations
=====================================================

Scope
-----
Video::ReelMagicStream prepares MPEG-1 assets for ScummVM's existing decoder.
It has no game-engine dependency. Callers choose a magic key and whether the
video needs unlocking. Script commands, scene addressing, playback policy and
presentation policy remain responsibilities of the consumer.
ReelMagicCompositor supplies integer graphics replication, palette keying and
video scaling; the engine selects the geometry and presents the result.

ReelMagic does not imply protected MPEG. The Crime Patrol and Drug Wars assets
examined for this work are ordinary MPEG-1 program streams, with legal frame
rate codes and no scrambled picture fields. They use byte-offset scene ranges
and the standard decoder rather than the unlock API. These observations apply
to the examined releases, not every game or every ReelMagic card.

Prior work and attribution
-------------------------
Jon Dennis (jrdennisoss) created DOSBox ReelMagic and documented the nonstandard
"magical MPEG-1" format and its recovery algorithm:

  https://github.com/jrdennisoss/dosboxrm
  https://github.com/jrdennisoss/dosboxrm/blob/main/NOTES_MPEG.md

His work, also available through DOSBox Staging, was the starting point for
this implementation. In particular, credit belongs to that work for the
reserved frame-rate marker, P/B-picture f_code scrambling, the known magic-key
patterns, and the temporal-sequence delta recovery algorithm. These are not
new discoveries of the ScummVM investigation. The DOSBox ReelMagic README
also credits Chris Guthrie and Joseph Whittaker as project contributors.

ScummVM adapts that documented format and algorithm to its stream interfaces
and existing MPEG decoder. Its buffering, optional per-picture decoding,
audio-padding repair and engine integration should be described separately
from the earlier format research. The sequence-header probe scans the first
16 KB of raw input; it does not
recognize a header split across program-stream packets. Unlocking itself
reassembles video payloads and repairs picture headers across those boundaries.

An unknown magic key still falls back to
the known default after a warning; arbitrary-key recovery is not implemented.

Card and CRT experiments, 2026-08-19
------------------------------------------
CALIB and CALIB2 displayed generated 320x240 MPEG patterns alongside 320x200
VGA artwork on a physical ReelMagic setup. The experiments used line
combs, numbered staircases, edge markers and adjacent MPEG/VGA vernier bands.
CRT photographs and scanline counts were compared with emulator
renders of the same patterns. This section records that investigation; it is
not a claim of a fresh hardware test for this PR.

The principal observations were:

* VGA features occupied two output scanlines. MPEG features could occupy one
  or two. MPEG therefore was not first reduced onto the VGA's 200-line logical
  grid and then doubled alongside it.
* The MPEG comb retained its cycles across the measured interior window. It
  did not show the periodic line loss of the examined emulator's 240-to-200
  path. Four narrow photo strips gave 98 cycles against approximately 97
  expected for their measured extent, within edge-counting uncertainty.
* The bottom staircase ended at MPEG source line 237. Its line-238/239 edge
  marker was absent, while the VGA staircase and bottom marker remained.
  This supports a two-source-line bottom crop on the tested setup. It does
  not justify claiming that all 240 source lines were visible.
* The monitor reported a 400-line VGA timing, labelled 720x400 at 70 Hz.
  That excludes the proposed 480-line output mode on this setup. The label
  alone does not establish the card's scaler geometry.

Interpretation and remaining uncertainty
---------------------------------------
The differing scanline granularity supports compositing video against the
already double-scanned VGA raster. It contradicts the earlier assumption that
the physical card discards every sixth MPEG line. That skipping path exists
in the examined DOSBox mixer; it is not a hardware measurement. Dennis's
README already identifies VGA/MPEG mixing architecture as work to revisit.

CALIB2's every-third-source-line vernier predominantly showed runs of thirteen
two-scanline bars and six one-scanline bars. A model of 32/19 output scanlines
per source line (19/32 source lines per output scanline) fits those run lengths
and predicts bottom overshoot. The recorded sequence includes a run counted
as sixteen rather than thirteen. The research notes treated it as a counting
error, but that interpretation has not been independently established here.
Describe 19/32 as the fitted model, not a directly read hardware register.

A five-fractional-bit truncation of the nominal 240/400 source step produces
19/32. That is a plausible register explanation, inferred from the model;
no register readback or datasheet measurement establishes it. The initial
photographs also cannot reliably establish horizontal scaling because of CRT
curvature and lens distortion.

Consequences for ScummVM
-----------------------
Keep the game's VGA artwork on an integer grid and put video resampling on the
video layer. The RtZ consumer selects a 640x400 destination, replicates
320x200 graphics by 2 on each axis and scales the complete 320x240 MPEG picture
into it. The shared compositor supports this geometry without depending on
MADE. Its ideal 3/5 source step preserves all 240 video lines instead of
reproducing the fitted model's bottom crop. That is an explicit presentation
choice. Backend aspect correction applies to the finished composite.

The 1600x1200 scaling experiment, deblocking and loop-skip preferences, and
restoration of omitted game sound cues remain separate local experiments.

Other findings from this investigation
--------------------------------------
Asset analysis found MPEG Layer II frames marked padded without a padding
byte. Correcting those header bits repaired 245 frames in the Zork intro.
This is an asset/decoder finding, not a measurement of the card's circuitry.
Likewise, a program-stream packet can contain several pictures: ScummVM's
opt-in per-picture decode path exposes each one and flushes delayed pictures
at EOF. That decoder behavior is useful beyond ReelMagic.

Calibration asset construction also mattered. The working calibration clips
used the game's ordinary bare MPEG-1 elementary-stream format and comparable
bitrate/VBV settings. The first high-rate, all-intra pattern was not a reliable
geometry probe because it produced corruption. File-open calibration differs
from Zork's callback-fed stream-open path, although it exercises the display
path; do not treat it as validation of callback/DMA behavior.
